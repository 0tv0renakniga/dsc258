WHAT ARE PART OF SPEECH TAGS?




  Parts-of-speech are syntactic classes of words
   • Not semantic

  Examples:
   • Coarse-grain: Noun, Verb, …
   • Fine-grain: Proper Noun, Pronoun, …

  The tags vary across languages
   • Think about those language-specific phenomena

    •   The (hidden) POS tag sequence s
    •   The (observed) word sequence w
    •   The dependency between adjacent words/tags (i.e., context)

INTRODUCING CONTEXT-FREE FEATURES



  Example features
   • Word
   • Lowercased word
   • Prefixes
   • Suffixes
   • Capitalization
   • Word shapes

  Build a classifier to estimate P(tag | word)
   • à can lead to 93.7% accuracy without context
MAKING IT CONTEXT-AWARE




  Conditional Random Field (CRF)

  High-level explanations
   • Extends the hidden states from discrete tags to continuous vector
       representations
   • Builds a classifier to predict the tag based on the vector representation
   • Decodes the tag sequence by considering the joint transition-emission
       probability distribution
UNSUPERVISED POS TAGGING



 Expectation-Maximization (EM)
  • Start with a uniform HMM
  • Run EM
  • Inspect results

 Clustering based on contexts
  • Distributional representation
  • Contextualized representations from neural language models

 • …
DOMAIN EFFECTS IN POS TAGGERS



   Accuracies degrade outside of domain
    • E.g., applies a tagger trained on news to tweets
    • Up to 3x error rate
    • Usually make the most errors on the things you care about in the
       domain (e.g., protein names)

      • This requires users to train their own domain-specific taggers
